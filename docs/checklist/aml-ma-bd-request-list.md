## Original requests

### AML-BD-001 — 1. Current Written Supervisory Procedures and AML Policies

Provide the firm’s current written supervisory procedures, AML policies and procedures, Customer Identification Program (CIP) procedures, customer due diligence (CDD) procedures, beneficial ownership procedures, OFAC/sanctions procedures, suspicious activity reporting procedures, and any related compliance manuals or supplements.

### AML-BD-002 — 2. Firm Representation Letter

Provide a representation letter on firm letterhead, signed by the AML Compliance Officer, Chief Compliance Officer, and President or other appropriate senior officer. The letter should state that, to their knowledge, they are not aware of suspicious activity or activity that would indicate money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, fraud, or other collusive activity by any employee, associated person, client, vendor, intermediary, affiliate, or other person in connection with the firm’s business, except as previously disclosed to the auditor.

### AML-BD-003 — 3. AML Training Program and Continuing Education Files

Provide the firm’s AML training program, training materials used during the audit period, training calendar, attendance records, completion records, attestations, testing results if used, and records of follow-up for incomplete or late training.

### AML-BD-004 — 4. Clearing Firm AML Reports – (If applicable)

Provide a list of all clearing-firm reports used as part of the firm’s AML program. For each report, identify its purpose, frequency, parameters, responsible reviewer, review and escalation procedures, and retention location. Provide samples for the audit period or, if more practical, the most recent three months.

### AML-BD-005 — 5. Other AML Reports and Checklists

Provide a list of all other reports, manual checklists, screening reports, exception reports, or other materials used as part of the firm’s AML, CDD, CIP, sanctions, or suspicious-activity-review processes. Identify the purpose, frequency, preparer, reviewer, and escalation process for each item.

### AML-BD-006 — Firm AML Systems and Technology

Provide written information regarding the computer systems, vendor platforms, screening tools, manual controls, and other technology used for the firm’s AML program. Identify a knowledgeable person available to discuss such systems and controls, if applicable.

### AML-BD-007 — 7. Current New-Engagement and Account-Opening Forms

Provide samples of a new-client, new-engagement, account-opening, advisory, investment-banking, engagement, due-diligence, CIP, CDD, beneficial ownership, sanctions-screening, and risk-rating forms currently in use.

### AML-BD-008 — 8. List of New Accounts and Engagements

Provide a list of all accounts opened and all actual or prospective M&A, capital-raising, fairness-opinion, or other investment-banking engagements accepted, terminated, or completed during the audit period. Include client name, engagement type, date, industry, domestic or foreign status, transaction value or expected value, and disposition.

### AML-BD-009 — New Client and Customer Due Diligence Information

For the engagements or accounts selected by the auditor from the list provided under Item 10, provide the complete new-client/account-opening and due-diligence file. This should include CIP, CDD, legal-entity documentation, beneficial ownership and control-person information, identity-verification records, sanctions-screening results, risk rating, exception documentation, approvals, and any enhanced due diligence performed. (This will be requested AFTER this initial form is submitted)

### AML-BD-010 — 10. General Ledger

Provide the general ledger for the audit period and, at a minimum, the most recent three months. Identify accounts used for advisory fees, retainers, success fees, referral fees, expense reimbursements, and other transaction-related receipts or disbursements.

### AML-BD-011 — 11. Foreign Accounts or Foreign Client Relationships

Provide a list of foreign accounts, foreign clients, non-U.S. legal entities, foreign beneficial owners, foreign counterparties, or other foreign relationships that engaged in business with the firm during the audit period. Include jurisdiction, engagement type, ownership information available to the firm, risk rating, and any enhanced due diligence or approval.

### AML-BD-012 — 12. Wire Transfers and Account Transfers

Provide reports or records of all customer and firm wire transfers, account transfers, journals, ACH activity, or other money-movement activity during the audit period, to the extent such activity is conducted by, through, or visible to the firm. If the firm does not handle, direct, or have visibility into such activity, provide a written representation describing the firm’s role and the extent of its access to such records.

### AML-BD-013 — 13. Regulatory and Government Correspondence

Provide copies of all correspondence, requests, examinations, inquiries, subpoenas, deficiency letters, referrals, settlements, or other communications during the audit period with FINRA, the SEC, the Department of the Treasury, FinCEN, OFAC, the IRS, law enforcement, state securities regulators, or any other state or federal regulatory agency, to the extent related to AML, sanctions, customer due diligence, suspicious activity, fraud, or financial crime.

### AML-BD-014 — 14. Internal AML Audit and Testing Records

Provide books and records relating to any internal audit, compliance testing, supervisory review, risk assessment, quality-assurance review, or other testing of the firm’s AML program, systems, controls, or related procedures.

### AML-BD-015 — 15. Frozen, Restricted, or Closed Accounts/Engagements

Provide a list of accounts concerning any frozen, restricted, rejected, declined, terminated, exited, or closed accounts or engagements due to AML, sanctions, CDD, CIP, fraud, suspicious-activity, or other financial-crime concerns.

### AML-BD-016 — 16. Private Banking Accounts

Provide a list of accounts concerning any private-banking accounts, if applicable. If the firm does not offer or maintain private-banking accounts, provide a written representation to that effect.

### AML-BD-017 — 17. SARs and Other Financial-Crime Filings

Provide a log of any Currency Transaction Reports, Suspicious Activity Reports, Suspicious Activity Report continuations, CMIRs, FBARs, Form 8300 filings, or similar reports made on behalf of the firm during the audit period and the prior two years, as applicable. Provide supporting documentation through a controlled process that preserves SAR confidentiality. Include documentation supporting decisions to file or not file a SAR, where maintained.

### AML-BD-018 — 18. FinCEN Requests and OFAC/FinCEN Screening

Provide evidence or books and records relating to any FinCEN requests, including Section 314(a) requests and responses, and evidence of applicable OFAC/sanctions and FinCEN-related screening or review procedures. Include related search logs, potential matches, research, dispositions, and escalation records.

### AML-BD-019 — 19. OFAC Screening System

Provide a description of, and access or demonstration as appropriate to, any system, vendor, process, or program used to screen new and existing clients, beneficial owners, control persons, associated persons, counterparties, and other relevant parties against the OFAC SDN List and other applicable sanctions lists. Include screening timing, list-update procedures, alert logs, potential-match review records, false-positive dispositions, and records of any blocked or rejected transactions.

### AML-BD-020 — 20. AML/CFT and Sanctions Risk Assessment

Provide the firm’s current AML/CFT and sanctions risk assessment and any prior version used during the audit period. Include the methodology, risk factors, risk ratings, approvals, updates, and documentation showing consideration of the firm’s M&A advisory activities, client types, transaction counterparties, transaction size, domestic and foreign exposure, ownership structures, fee arrangements, payment practices, intermediaries, products and services, and third-party/vendor dependencies.

### AML-BD-021 — 21. Independent AML Testing and Remediation

Provide the most recent independent AML test report and all reports issued during the audit period. Include the audit scope, testing checklist or workpapers, tester qualifications, documentation of independence, findings, management responses, remediation plan, responsible parties, target dates, and evidence that corrective actions were completed and validated.

### AML-BD-022 — 22. Transaction Counterparty Due Diligence, If Applicable

If the firm conducts, obtains, or relies on due diligence regarding material transaction counterparties, buyers, sellers, investors, lenders, acquirers, affiliates, or intermediaries, provide the written procedures and records of such review for engagements during the audit period. Include sanctions screening, ownership information, enhanced diligence, escalation, and approval records, if any. If not applicable, provide a written representation describing the firm’s role and why such review is not performed.

### AML-BD-023 — 23. Firm Fee and Payment Exceptions

Provide a schedule of advisory fees, retainers, success fees, referral fees, consulting fees, and expense reimbursements received or paid by the firm during the audit period. Identify any payment that was made by or to a person other than the contracting client or approved payee; involved a foreign or offshore account; was split among multiple parties; varied materially from the governing agreement; involved unusual payment instructions; or required an exception, escalation, or enhanced-diligence review. Provide supporting documentation for each identified exception.

### AML-BD-024 — 24. Intermediary and Referral Arrangements, If Applicable

If the firm used any finder, referral source, consultant, placement agent, introducer, joint-venture partner, or other third-party intermediary in connection with an engagement during the audit period, provide the applicable agreements, compensation arrangements, due-diligence records, ownership information, sanctions-screening evidence, and approval documentation. If none, provide a written representation that no such arrangements existed during the audit period.

### AML-BD-025 — 25. AML Escalations and SAR Decisioning

Provide all AML, sanctions, fraud, suspicious-activity, or other financial-crime escalations relating to prospective or actual engagements during the audit period. Include matters that were declined, withdrawn, delayed, terminated, restricted, or referred to the AML Compliance Officer, Compliance, Legal, senior management, outside counsel, law enforcement, FINRA, the SEC, OFAC, or FinCEN. Provide the SAR log, continuing-activity review records, and supporting decision documentation through a controlled process that preserves SAR confidentiality.

### AML-BD-026 — 26. Section 314(a) and Government Requests

Provide records of all FinCEN Section 314(a) searches and responses, FinCEN requests, law-enforcement inquiries, subpoenas, grand-jury subpoenas, regulatory requests, and related escalation, preservation, response, and record-retention documentation during the audit period.

### AML-BD-027 — 27. AML Controls Inventory

Provide a description of each manual and automated AML, CDD, CIP, sanctions, and suspicious-activity control used in the firm’s M&A business. For each control, identify the control objective, responsible person, frequency, input or data source, evidence retained, escalation process, reviewer or approver, system or vendor used, and any exceptions identified during the audit period.

### AML-BD-028 — 28. Material Changes Since Prior Audit

Provide a summary of material changes since the prior AML audit or independent AML test, including changes in ownership, management, AML personnel, associated persons, business model, services, transaction size or type, client profile, foreign-jurisdiction exposure, payment practices, vendors, clearing or banking relationships, systems, policies, procedures, sanctions-screening tools, regulatory findings, or disciplinary history. Include related AML impact assessments, approvals, and revised procedures.

### AML-BD-029 — 29. AML Governance and Senior Management Oversight

Provide written evidence of senior-management approval of the AML program; AML Compliance Officer designation; AML Compliance Officer responsibilities, authority, and reporting line; any AML committee or management meeting minutes; AML-related management reports; and records of senior-management review of material AML, sanctions, suspicious-activity, audit, testing, or remediation matters during the audit period.

### AML-BD-030 — 30. Vendor and Third-Party AML Control Oversight

Provide a list of vendors and third parties supporting AML, CIP, CDD, sanctions screening, client onboarding, transaction monitoring, recordkeeping, or related functions. For each material vendor, provide applicable contracts, due-diligence records, risk assessments, service descriptions, control reports or certifications if available, incident or outage records, change notices, and evidence of periodic oversight.

### AML-BD-031 — 31. AML Record Retention and Access Controls

Provide the firm’s record-retention schedule and procedures applicable to AML, CIP, CDD, beneficial ownership, OFAC, sanctions screening, Section 314(a), SAR-related, and audit records. Provide user-access listings for relevant AML or screening systems and evidence of periodic access review, including removal of access for departed personnel, if applicable.

### AML-BD-032 — 32. Business Continuity for AML Controls

Provide procedures and evidence addressing continuity of AML, sanctions-screening, and suspicious-activity escalation controls during system outages, vendor disruptions, staffing absences, or other business-continuity events. Include any relevant outage or incident logs and compensating-control documentation during the audit period.

### AML-BD-033 — 33. AML Staffing and Segregation of Duties

Provide an organizational chart and identify personnel with responsibilities for client onboarding, CIP, CDD, beneficial ownership, sanctions screening, engagement approval, transaction review, AML escalation, SAR decisioning, training, and AML testing. Describe segregation-of-duties controls and backup coverage for key AML functions.

### AML-BD-034 — 34. Compliance Complaints, Investigations, and Discipline

Provide a log of complaints, internal investigations, disciplinary actions, whistleblower or hotline matters, fraud allegations, cybersecurity/account-takeover matters, and compliance concerns during the audit period that involved or could have implicated AML, sanctions, client identity, beneficial ownership, suspicious activity, or financial crime. Provide relevant supporting records for items selected by the auditor.
